Two Modules, One Cool(ing) Decision

Should a portable communications unit use powered ventilation or a passive thermal bridge to stay within its operating-temperature limits?

The power module and control module generate heat at different rates. Their housings lose heat to ambient air, while a contact transfers heat from the hotter module to the cooler one. Use energy balances, a linear differential system, and its eigenmodes to make a recommendation. All equipment and measurements are synthetic teaching data.

01 · Account for energy

One component: heat in, heat out

With thermal capacity C, internal heat rate P, ambient-loss coefficient h, and ambient temperature Ta, the balance is C T′ = P − h(T − Ta). The right side determines the instantaneous direction of temperature change.

Predict: what is T′(0)? If the module began above its equilibrium temperature, which way would it move?

02 · Follow the solution
Watch an exponential approach to equilibrium
Set θ = T − Ta. At equilibrium, θ* = P/h. Subtracting θ* leaves z′ = −(h/C)z; an exponential ansatz gives T(t) = Ta + P/h + [T(0) − Ta − P/h]e−ht/C. The time constant is τ = C/h minutes. The departure from equilibrium is multiplied by e−t/τ. After one time constant, about 63% of the initial temperature gap has closed and 37% remains. For the supplied inputs, τ = 10 minutes and T(10) ≈ 22.53°C, approaching 24°C.
Predict: where will the curve level off, and how does greater ventilation change it?

04 · Read the eigenmodes
Overall elevation and temperature contrast
Write θᵢ = Tᵢ − Ta. The sum s = θ₁ + θ₂ tracks overall elevation; the difference d = θ₁ − θ₂ tracks contrast. The modes have eigenvectors (1, 1)T and (1, −1)T. Bridge exchange cancels in the sum equation and acts on contrast.
Predict: which mode decays faster when g > 0? What might increasing g do to the control temperature?
