Mathematical Sciences · Project 1

Finding a Defensible Split

Can a collaboration graph help a program director form two seminar cohorts of three teams?

Build a graph from its adjacency matrix, derive its degree and Laplacian matrices, inspect eigen-information, and count collaborations separated by a proposed split. The mathematics informs a recommendation; it does not make the decision for you.

Download worksheet PDFOpen graph laboratoryRead guided inquiry

Teaching case: all team and collaboration data are synthetic. An edge records a tie, not its quality, strength, or future effect. Do not enter real personnel or sensitive information.

Start with a prediction

  1. Download the worksheet PDF and draw the case graph by hand.
  2. Load the six-team case. The graph and its adjacency matrix represent the same eight edges.
  3. Work one step at a time. Predict a degree or matrix row before asking Python to calculate it.
  4. Interpret the eigen-output, then form cohorts yourself and audit the crossing edges.
  5. Change an edge to test how dependent your advice is on the recorded graph. Export your evidence before leaving the page.

06 · Challenge

Change the record, then reassess

The packet asks what happens if ties {1,5} and {3,6} were omitted. These controls add those edges to the six-team case; they do not erase earlier comparisons. Hold each candidate partition fixed and recount its cut. After a graph change, matrix and eigen-results are marked out of date until recalculated.

A changed edge record may change the eigenvector too. For the packet's stress test, compare the fixed office and original sign-based partitions; do not silently reinterpret an old eigenvector as the new one.
