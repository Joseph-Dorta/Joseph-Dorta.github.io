Two Modules, One Cool(ing) Decision

Guided inquiry and individual writing assignment

Work through the questions with your partners during class. Record your reasoning in the worksheet PDF or your own notes. The thermal laboratory checks one mathematical step at a time after you make a prediction.

Decision brief: a fictional portable communications unit has a heat-producing power module and a temperature-sensitive control module. Its director can install one modification: powered ventilation or a passive thermal bridge. Recommend one, a limited test, or deferral. Connect your claim to predicted temperatures, physical reasoning, and evidence limits. All data are synthetic.

Course connections: Lesson 17, eigenvalues and eigenvectors; Lesson 18, diagonalization and eigenvector coordinates; Lessons 19 and 20, linear ODE systems and modeling.

1. From heat flow to one differential equation

When the unit switches on, the electronics produce heat at rate P kJ/min. The module's thermal capacity C kJ/°C describes the energy needed for a one-degree temperature rise. Heat travels through the housing and into surrounding air. The Newton cooling approximation represents that ambient heat flow as h(T − Ta), where h has units kJ/(min·°C). The signed term reverses if the module is cooler than the ambient air. We represent the module with one temperature T(t).

Stored-energy rate equals heat produced minus heat lost:

C T′ = P − h(T − Ta).

At a constant ambient temperature, set θ = T − Ta. The equilibrium elevation is θ* = P/h. The departure z = θ − θ* satisfies z′ = −(h/C)z. Trying z = Keλt gives λ = −h/C and

T(t) = Ta + P/h + [T(0) − Ta − P/h]e−ht/C.

Example: with C = 10, P = 4, h = 1, Ta = 20°C, and T(0) = 20°C, the solution is T(t) = 24 − 4e−t/10 °C. The time constant τ = C/h is 10 minutes. Since the departure from equilibrium contains e−t/τ, after τ minutes about 37% of the initial gap remains and 63% has closed. Here, T(10) ≈ 22.53°C on the way from 20°C toward 24°C.

  1. Find T′(0) in °C/min. What physical process changes this slope later?
  2. Evaluate T(10). What fraction of the initial gap to 24°C remains after one time constant (10 minutes)? Explain using the exponential factor.
  3. Suppose T(0) exceeds equilibrium. How does the sign of the exponential coefficient change, and how does T move?

Calculate the balance, then inspect the curve.

2. Couple two modules through heat transfer

Let T₁ denote the power-module temperature and T₂ the control-module temperature. They produce heat at rates P₁ and P₂, respectively. Each loses heat to ambient air through its housing. The bridge conducts heat at the signed rate g(T₁ − T₂) from module 1 toward module 2. We initially assume equal capacities C and equal ambient-loss coefficients h.

With θᵢ = Tᵢ − Ta, the first balance is Cθ₁′ = P₁ − hθ₁ − g(θ₁ − θ₂).

  1. Write the balance for module 2. Explain the sign of its contact term.
  2. Add the balances. Which terms cancel, and what does that say about a bridge's ability to remove energy from the whole unit?
  3. If T₁ = T₂ > Ta, which heat flow is zero? Which continue?

After deriving both balances, check their matrix form:

θ⃗′ = Aθ⃗ + b⃗,   A = (1/C) [−(h+g)   g;   g   −(h+g)],   b⃗ = (1/C)(P₁,P₂)ᵀ.

The matrix A has units min⁻¹ and b⃗ has units °C/min. The baseline uses C = 10, h = 1, g = 0.5, P₁ = 20, P₂ = 4, Ta = 20°C, and T₁(0) = T₂(0) = 20°C.

  1. Fill numerical A and b⃗. Check the units of one term in each equation.
  2. Find T₁′(0) and T₂′(0). Is contact heat flowing at power-on? What changes immediately afterward?

Construct the matrix with Python after your derivation.

3. See eigenvectors as temperature patterns

Constant heat production adds b⃗ to the homogeneous system. An equilibrium θ⃗* satisfies Aθ⃗* + b⃗ = 0. The displacement z⃗ = θ⃗ − θ⃗* then satisfies z⃗′ = Az⃗. Its eigenmodes describe the fading departures from equilibrium even when both absolute temperatures rise from ambient.

Define s = θ₁ + θ₂ (overall elevation) and d = θ₁ − θ₂ (contrast). Derive, then check:

s′ = −(h/C)s + (P₁ + P₂)/C;   d′ = −[(h + 2g)/C]d + (P₁ − P₂)/C.

The bridge terms cancel in s′ and reinforce each other in d′. The eigenvectors are v⃗₊ = (1,1)ᵀ and v⃗₋ = (1,−1)ᵀ.

  1. Why does increasing g speed the decay of contrast while leaving the common-mode decay rate unchanged in this symmetric model?
  2. Multiply A by each eigenvector. Find λ₊ and λ₋, including units.
  3. For the baseline, compare eλ₊t and eλ₋t. Which departure from equilibrium fades faster?
  4. Find s* and d*, then convert them to baseline equilibrium temperatures. Explain why T₁* exceeds T₂*.
  5. Write θ⃗(t) = θ⃗* + c₊eλ₊tv⃗₊ + c₋eλ₋tv⃗₋. Determine c₊ and c₋ from θ⃗(0) = 0⃗.

For a scalar equation y′ = −ay + f, the solution is y(t) = y* + [y(0) − y*]e−at. With both modules initially at ambient:

s(t) = [(P₁+P₂)/h](1−e−ht/C),   d(t) = [(P₁−P₂)/(h+2g)](1−e−(h+2g)t/C).

Then θ₁ = (s+d)/2 and θ₂ = (s−d)/2. Inspect eigenmodes and equilibrium after you make a prediction.

4. Compare modifications at the relevant time

The planned operation lasts 30 minutes at 20°C ambient. The limits are T₁ ≤ 34.5°C and T₂ ≤ 29.0°C. Exactly one modification may be installed.

CasehgOperational consideration
Baseline10.5No added load
Ventilation1.350.525 W draw; filter upkeep
Bridge11.0Passive; added mass and uncertain contact quality

In the model, ventilation motor heat leaves outside the two modules. P₁ and P₂ remain fixed. For the baseline at t = 30, ht/C = 3 and (h+2g)t/C = 6, so s(30) = 24(1−e−3) and d(30) = 8(1−e−6).

  1. Compute baseline T₁(30) and T₂(30) by hand. Which limit matters?
  2. Predict the effect of increasing h on overall elevation and increasing g on contrast. Could the bridge warm the control module?
  3. Record T₁(30), T₂(30), and both equilibrium temperatures for each case. Carry unrounded values through the calculation.
  4. Which cases meet both 30-minute limits? Could an equilibrium comparison yield a different decision? Explain its physical meaning.
  5. Why do negative eigenvalues alone not determine whether a temperature limit is met?

Forecast the three cases one at a time and export your completed evidence.

6. Turn exploration into a decision memo

Before leaving class, record your own provisional claim, decision criterion, ODE and temperature evidence, physical limitation or contrary evidence, and next measurement. A conditional recommendation is appropriate when a prediction lies close to a limit or depends on an untested heat-transfer assumption.

Final product (typed): an independently authored Army-style memo of at most two pages to the program director. Recommend ventilation, a bridge, a conditional trial, or deferral. State the 30-minute criterion, numerical comparison, ventilation power tradeoff, and a limitation in language a nontechnical leader can use. Label modeled predictions and measured outcomes clearly.

Attach a separate technical annex of at most three pages with the heat-balance derivation and units; A, b⃗, and the initial state; equilibrium and one eigenpair check; a labeled temperature figure or table; residuals; and hotter-day sensitivity. Your instructor will provide the due date and submission method.

Approved AI may coach you during the designated class inquiry if you document and verify its help. Write the final memo and annex by your own hand, without generative AI outlining, drafting, rewriting, editing, or creating figure captions. Typing and the supplied calculator are allowed.

Final physical check: explain why increasing g can cool the hot module while warming the cooler module, whereas increasing h removes energy from the whole unit.
