Keeping a Fleet Ready

Guided inquiry and individual writing assignment

Work collaboratively in class; final writing is individual. Record your responses in the packet or your own notes. This web version presents the same case and inquiry prompts without the packet's writing spaces.

The decision: a closed fleet has 100 vehicles: 30 ready, 40 in routine maintenance, and 30 in major repair. A training event is two weeks away. The planning criterion is at least 70 expected-ready vehicles at the end of week 2. Exactly one proposal can receive the same fictional one-time $20,000 budget: prevention reduces entry into maintenance from the ready state; faster repairs improves return-to-ready transitions.

Advise which proposal, if either, the evidence supports for this deadline. Explain whether a longer planning horizon could change the decision. All records and estimates are synthetic teaching data, not operational evidence.

50-minute plan: understand the case (8); construct the model (10); investigate with the notebook (12); challenge the evidence (12); prepare a claim and save work (8).

1. Separate the decision, data, and assumptions

  1. Who is your audience? What quantity and deadline will you use to compare proposals?
  2. What is the difference between 70 expected-ready vehicles and a guarantee of 70 actual ready vehicles?

State order: Ready, Routine maintenance, Major repair. The pooled records contain these current-week to next-week transitions:

2. Translate the case into linear algebra

xk+1 = A xk   (3 × 1) = (3 × 3)(3 × 1)

The entry aij is the observed number of transitions from origin j to destination i divided by all observed transitions from origin j.

  1. Construct the baseline matrix's first column. Explain its entries in words.
  2. Construct the whole baseline matrix A0. What should each column sum to? Why?
  3. Calculate x1 = A0(30, 40, 30)T by hand. Show the dot product for the ready coordinate. Check the total.

Assume constant rates, immediate effects, exclusive and exhaustive states, and transitions that depend only on the current state. No vehicles enter or leave the fleet.

  1. Which entries differ from baseline, and how do those changes represent each proposal?
  2. Predict before computing: which proposal might help most at week 2? Which might help most eventually? Give a reason; a wrong prediction is not a grading penalty.

3. Use the notebook as an analytical instrument

Open the notebook from the OR pilot page, run its cells unchanged to display the controls, keep the supplied inputs for the first run, and click Run analysis. Coding is not required.

  • xk = Akx0 gives the finite-horizon forecast. Repeated multiplication gives the same forecast.
  • If A = PDP−1, then Ak = PDkP−1. Eigenvalues describe how eigenvector modes change with time.
  • A normalized eigenvector p with Ap = p and coordinates summing to 1 is a stationary distribution. For these default matrices, the other eigenvalues have magnitude below 1, so the model approaches this distribution.
  • A numerical residual checks a calculation; it does not establish that the model represents actual fleet behavior.

For baseline, prevention, and faster repairs, record the week-2 expected ready count, whether the target is met in expectation, the long-term expected ready count, and the run ID.

  1. Explain the eigenvalue 1 and its eigenvector in this case. Why should a steady-state vector not replace a week-2 forecast?
  2. Use the ready-versus-week plot to identify when the proposals' ordering changes. Relate this to your predictions.
  3. Record a numerical check and its result. What exactly does it verify?

Save evidence: click Export last run and retain the ZIP and run ID. Exports describe the last completed run, not unrun changes to controls.

B. A sensitivity test

Set both effect sliders to 50%, leaving all other inputs unchanged. The tool interpolates between baseline and proposal:

A(f) = (1 − f)A0 + f Aproposal,   0 ≤ f ≤ 1.

This is a hypothetical partial-effect scenario, not a confidence interval.

  1. Record each proposal's week-2 expected readiness. Does either still meet the criterion?
  2. Choose one additional test: deadline, starting fleet mix totaling 100, or effectiveness. Predict its effect, run it, and record settings and run ID.
  3. What assumption or missing evidence most limits your recommendation?

5. From evidence to an independently authored memo

Before leaving class, record your own claim, supporting evidence, limitation, and next information needed. Submit the inquiry packet and save default and sensitivity evidence bundles.

Final individual product (typed)

  • Memo: at most two pages. Use the instructor's Army-style draft, addressed to the notional maintenance officer. Include a clear bottom line, a comparison, a labeled figure or table, a limitation, and what could change your recommendation. Explain results in language a nontechnical leader can use.
  • Technical annex: at most three pages. Define the state vector and matrix convention; show the data-to-matrix construction and a hand calculation; explain matrix powers and eigenvalue methods; document a numerical verification, baseline holdout check, and sensitivity results with run IDs. Provide enough detail for a technical reader to reproduce your conclusion.
  • Document sources, classroom assistance, and AI use. Inquiry and computational evidence are outside the five-page writing limit.

AI boundary: AI assistance is permitted only during designated classroom inquiry. The final memo and annex must be independently authored: no generative AI outlining, drafting, rewriting, editing, or caption generation, and no copied AI prose from class. The notebook and its numerical/graphical outputs remain permitted. Ordinary formatting and non-generative spell-check are permitted.

Do not attach the notebook evidence record as a substitute for your technical explanation.

Include this acknowledgment:

Strong work demonstrates: correct linear-algebra representation; contextual interpretation; evaluation of alternatives; verification and appropriately limited validation claims; sensitivity to assumptions; clear communication to both audiences. A well-supported conditional recommendation is preferable to unwarranted certainty.
